PARTCONCEPTS

A Unified Mechanism for Fine-Grained Part Localization and Generation

PARTCONCEPTS encodes textual part descriptions (e.g. ‘left ear’) into compact tokens trained to attend to the correct part region in an image. The resulting localization sets a new state-of-the-art on open-vocabulary part segmentation, and directly enables fine-grained, part-level instruction following in text-to-image generation.

Abstract

While text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models exhibit strong semantic disentanglement at the object level, they struggle to localize fine-grained object parts. Although the latent visual features in these models are sufficiently fine-grained, the text–image interaction (cross-attention) fails to effectively exploit this information. This results in coarse and ambiguous localization, particularly for spatially distinct components such as ‘left’ and ‘right’ limbs.

To address this limitation, we introduce PARTCONCEPTS, a mechanism that encodes textual part descriptions into compact, learnable representations. These PARTCONCEPT tokens are trained to selectively attend to their corresponding part regions in the image. To evaluate the part-level understanding of PARTCONCEPT tokens, we first probe their effectiveness for part segmentation. We then evaluate whether the improved localization enables fine-grained instruction following in T2I generation.

On the standard Open-Vocabulary Part Segmentation (OVPS) benchmark, our method outperforms dedicated segmentation baselines, establishing a new state-of-the-art. We further evaluate on the more complex Pascal-Part benchmark for part instance segmentation, which contains fine-grained spatial annotations (e.g. ‘left lower arm’) — here we outperform all baselines by a very significant margin. Finally, our qualitative and quantitative results show that this strong localization directly enables fine-grained instruction following in T2I generation: textual attributes (e.g., color) inherently bind to the correct PARTCONCEPT, without any explicit attribute-control mechanism. This indicates that PARTCONCEPT tokens compose seamlessly with other text tokens, highlighting their applicability for fine-grained, text-based generative control.

Method

Given an input text prompt with part phrases (e.g., ‘left ear’), our goal is to make those phrases attend strongly to the correct part regions in the image — enabling both precise part-level localization and, consequently, fine-grained instruction following in T2I generation.

3.1 · The PARTCONCEPT encoder

Encoding textual part descriptions into PARTCONCEPTs

Cross-attention maps from FLUX.2 show that part phrases tokenize into discrete tokens that attend coarsely to disparate image regions — spatial descriptors like ‘left’ and ‘right’ are especially diffuse. We instead replace each part phrase with a single PARTCONCEPT token, encoded by a lightweight extension of the base text encoder. Because only a LoRA is trained on top of layers initialized from the base encoder, the PARTCONCEPT embedding does not drift far from the text embedding space — which is what lets it preserve the model's generative priors while gaining strong spatial grounding.

3.2 · Training objective

The localization and generative objectives

To keep the base model's generative capability intact, training combines the localization loss with the standard flow-matching (denoising) loss: L = L_dice + λ · L_flow. The weight λ trades off localization sharpness against generation fidelity (see paper for the full ablation study).

Results

We evaluate PARTCONCEPTS along two axes: how well it localizes parts (open-vocabulary and instance-level part segmentation), and how that localization translates into fine-grained instruction following in T2I generation.

Qualitative results

PARTCONCEPTS generalizes to novel part phrases unseen in training (armrest, left front wheel, wrist) and to arbitrary attributes — even in multi-part prompts, each attribute binds to its correct part. Dedicated segmentation models, meanwhile, struggle to separate spatially-distinct instances such as ‘left lower arm’ and ‘right upper arm’. 4.2 · Fine-grained part instance segmentation

Standard datasets used for OVPS turn out to be too coarse for precise grounding: they remove spatial descriptors and collapse distinct instances such as ‘left hand’ and ‘right hand’ into a single ‘hand’ class. The original Pascal-Part benchmark retains these fine-grained, spatially-identified instances, and is a substantially harder test of spatial disambiguation — here PARTCONCEPTS outperforms all baselines by a large margin.
